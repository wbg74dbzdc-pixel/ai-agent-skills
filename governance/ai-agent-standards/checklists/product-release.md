# Product Release and Legal Review

Use before public launch, monetization, collection of personal data, enabling user-generated content, or a material change in product behavior. This is issue-spotting and release governance, not legal advice. Applicability depends on facts and jurisdiction.

## Establish the facts first

- [ ] Record operator/business location, target markets, user locations, product platforms, intended and likely audiences, age range, and accessibility commitments.
- [ ] Map personal and sensitive data, purposes, sources, recipients, vendors, model providers, transfers, retention, logs, analytics, cookies, and deletion paths.
- [ ] Identify accounts, payments, subscriptions, advertising, recommendations, automated decisions, AI, health/financial claims, messaging, public content, moderation, and safety-sensitive features.
- [ ] Identify third-party content, code, assets, fonts, music, datasets, model outputs, trademarks, open-source licenses, and platform/store terms.
- [ ] Separate legal requirements, platform/contract requirements, voluntary standards, and product commitments. Do not present one as another.

## Applicability and policy-to-product parity

- [ ] Use the [compliance matrix](../templates/compliance-matrix.md) to record each issue, whether it applies and why, jurisdiction/platform, current authoritative source, control, evidence, owner, status, and review date.
- [ ] Check current official regulator, legislature, standards-body, platform, and contract sources for every target jurisdiction and distribution channel.
- [ ] Assess privacy notices, lawful basis/consent, user rights, retention/deletion/export, cookies/SDKs, international transfers, security duties, and breach procedures where relevant.
- [ ] Assess child/teen users, age assurance, parental consent, age-appropriate design, advertising, safety, and communication controls where relevant.
- [ ] Assess accessibility laws and procurement/platform requirements. For web content, select and test a specific WCAG conformance target; do not use “accessible” as an unverified claim.
- [ ] Assess consumer terms, accurate pricing, subscriptions, renewal/cancellation, refunds, warranties, fulfillment, seller identity, taxes, and required disclosures where applicable.
- [ ] Assess user-content ownership/licensing, moderation, notice/report/takedown, appeals, abuse, defamation, and record-preservation obligations where applicable.
- [ ] Assess game ratings, content disclosures, virtual currencies, randomized purchases, trading, gambling/real-money features, chat, and platform policies when relevant.
- [ ] Assess AI transparency, automated decisions, biometrics, high-impact uses, human oversight, records, and appeals where relevant.
- [ ] Verify rights and compatible licenses for code, media, fonts, data, marks, and AI-generated material; preserve attribution and required notices.
- [ ] Substantiate explicit and implied marketing claims. Use genuine reviews and disclose material connections; avoid deceptive interfaces and fabricated testimonials.
- [ ] Ensure every notice, term, consent, permission, privacy label, store listing, and marketing statement matches the actual released behavior and vendors.

## Go/no-go and continuing obligations

- [ ] Resolve uncertain, high-impact, or jurisdiction-specific legal questions with a qualified professional before launch; record the advice and scope without treating it as universal.
- [ ] Keep platform submission, age-rating, payment, export, and regional restrictions current for each release.
- [ ] Name owners for support, complaints, access/deletion requests, moderation, vulnerability reports, incidents, backups, and product shutdown.
- [ ] Verify contact channels, user notice/update processes, data deletion, account closure, refunds/cancellation, restore, and rollback actually work.
- [ ] Record release version, evidence, limitations, accepted residual risks, accountable approver, and next review trigger/date.
- [ ] Re-open review when target markets, audience, monetization, data, vendors, AI capability, user content, or platform distribution changes.

Examples of official starting points include the [FTC Children's Privacy guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/childrens-privacy), the [FTC reviews and endorsements guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews), and the relevant platform or regional regulator's own materials. These examples are U.S.-specific where indicated; identify actual applicable jurisdictions rather than applying them everywhere.
